Trials that enroll patients with confirmed, refractory, or histologically verified chronic conditions—especially those combining systemic anticancer agents with radiotherapy or requiring fixed-dose, multi-cycle intravenous chemotherapy—tend to be serious, as do protocols that mandate ECOG performance thresholds, numeric lab/imaging criteria, or stable ongoing treatment with no expected changes. In contrast, trials that include healthy volunteers, require cognitive or neurological assessments, or focus on pain severity, substance use disorders, or illicit drug use overwhelmingly end in none, as do those with ECG/imaging safety exclusions or washout periods for concomitant medications. To predict the outcome of a new protocol, a reader should first check whether enrollment is restricted to a specific chronic disease population with prior treatment history or refractory status, since this is the strongest single discriminator for seriousness. Next, look for the presence of any substance-use, psychiatric, or healthy-volunteer eligibility criteria, as these signals almost always indicate a non-serious trial regardless of other design features.
