Trials that end in high tend to be tightly controlled, dose-finding studies using branded agents, with strict quantitative thresholds, severe disease criteria, and explicit exclusions for psychiatric or substance-use comorbidities, often referencing prior work and long-term or long-acting contexts. Trials that end in low tend to rely on investigator discretion, local lab confirmation, and broad exclusions for concurrent medications or ECG/imaging abnormalities, while also restricting participants based on medication interference or abnormal organ function. A reader should look for whether a protocol emphasizes fixed dosing, branded products, and quantitative cutoffs (high) versus subjective judgment, local procedures, and medication-related safety exclusions (low).
