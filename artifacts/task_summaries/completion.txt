Trials that terminate are those whose enrollment hinges on anatomical accessibility, measurable lesion thresholds, or conditional test-based criteria, and that require standardized diagnostic scales, DSM-based substance use disorder definitions, or washout periods—signals that the population is narrowly selected and the design is feasibility-driven. Trials that complete are those that specify a commercial drug product by brand and manufacturer, mandate fixed named-intervention doses, compare at least two proprietary drugs, or exclude prior immunosuppressive/biologic use—signals of a mature, industry-backed efficacy or safety design. A reader should first check whether the protocol names a proprietary drug product and manufacturer, because that single marker most strongly predicts completion, whereas any eligibility criterion tied to a lesion’s reachability or a numeric imaging threshold most strongly predicts termination. The composite reading is that termination reflects restrictive, procedure-dependent patient selection and standardized assessment burdens, while completion reflects fixed, commercially anchored interventions and comparative efficacy frameworks.
